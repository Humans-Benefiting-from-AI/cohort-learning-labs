What am I actually like to think with?

You want to learn about yourself and others. We organize virtual meetings of six to eight members, where the goal is an environment that encourages learning about ourselves and others, experimentation with new behaviors, and interpersonal freedom.

No curriculum to get through. No agreement to arrive at. A recurring group, a facilitator who keeps the inquiry open, and whatever the six of you turn out to be like together.

01 — The individual

You are the one thing in the room you cannot see from the outside.

You already have positions, reactions, a rhythm of speaking, and a particular way of going quiet. You have been doing all of it for years. Almost none of it is visible to you, because you are the one doing it.

Reading about it changes nothing. One person’s feedback is easy to file away. What actually moves is watching it happen, live, among people curious enough to say what they noticed and unattached enough to be honest about it.

So the work starts with you and your own difference of opinion. The group is how you get to see it.

02 — Open questions

These are the questions a group keeps returning to. None of them have a settled answer.

  • 01What do I do with a difference of opinion when it first appears?
  • 02Do I soften what I think, and who taught me to?
  • 03Am I heard the way I intend to be heard?
  • 04What happens in me when someone disagrees and does not back down?
  • 05Which people make me quieter, and what is that about?
  • 06Can I understand a position without moving toward agreeing with it?
  • 07What do I do when the group starts forming a side?
  • 08What am I like to be in a group with?

03 — The laboratory

A group of six is the smallest place where all of it shows.

Between two people, a disagreement looks like a contest. Add a third and the structure appears: someone agrees for a different reason, someone waits, a majority forms, and the person left in the minority decides whether it is still worth saying. Nothing has to be invented. The group is already producing the material.

  • ObservationYou watch it happen while it is happening, instead of reconstructing it afterward from memory.
  • ExperimentYou try a way of speaking you would not risk at work, and find out what it actually does to people.
  • EvidenceFive other people notice things about you that you cannot generate on your own, in any quantity.
  • PracticeThe same faces, week after week, so a new behavior has somewhere to become ordinary.
  • ContainerConsequences are suspended. Nothing said here has to be managed for the rest of the week.

04 — A session

Ninety minutes. No exercise, no material we brought in. A note from one hour of one group:

00:07Someone raises a decision they are stuck on.Two people answer immediately. Three do not.
00:19A second member disagrees, then apologizes for disagreeing.The apology arrives before anyone objected.
00:31A third agrees with the first — for an entirely different reason.Now it looks like a majority. It is not one.
00:48The quietest member says the thing everyone was circling.The room reorganizes around it.
01:14The group notices what it just did.This is the part that does not happen alone.

The question the facilitator is holding the whole time is not what the group should decide. It is how each person is communicating a difference of opinion right now, and what that is doing to everyone else.

05 — Problems and struggles

The most unconventional thing asked of you here is to stop trying to get rid of the problem.

The ordinary instinct with a struggle is to solve it quickly or route around it. That instinct is reasonable and it works often enough. It also throws away the one thing the struggle was carrying: information about where you are not looking.

A disagreement that keeps coming back, a conversation the group keeps not having, the moment you go quiet and cannot say why — none of these are obstacles standing in front of the work. They are the work pointing at itself.

So the struggle becomes the path. Data to read, and a teacher telling you where to look and what to ask next.

Nothing has to be fixed before the inquiry can start. The difficulty is where it starts.

06 — What changes

Not a technique for winning agreement. A wider range of things you are able to do with people.

  • 01Say what you actually think, without pretending to speak for everyone.
  • 02Name the precise point of difference instead of opposing an entire person.
  • 03Represent someone else’s view accurately before you answer it.
  • 04Notice your own reaction and stay in the room anyway.
  • 05Tell the difference between understanding someone and agreeing with them.
  • 06Reach a decision without erasing what is still unresolved.

07 — Facilitation

Elie Schulman does not resolve the group’s differences for it.

His work is to protect the conditions in which a group can meet its differences and stay curious about them a little longer than is comfortable — including the ones nobody planned to raise.

He listens for what was said, what was avoided, and what the group is doing with a difference as it appears. He is responsible for the inquiry. You stay responsible for your positions, your choices, and your relationships.

08 — Ways to join

Open cohort

You join six or seven people you have never met.

No shared employer, no shared history, no stake in each other’s next promotion. Nothing you try has to be managed for the rest of the week, which is precisely what makes trying anything possible.

Schedule a consultation

Private group

Or you bring the group you are already in.

A team, a leadership group, a board, a faculty. The differences are live and the consequences are real, so the work is making what already happens between you examinable instead of only survivable.

09 — What is at stake

“The important thing is to connect.”

E.M. Forster

Forster wrote it about people kept apart by class, temperament, and a shortage of imagination for each other’s lives.

The failure he named is not dramatic. It is ordinary. Two people talk and something does not carry across. A group decides and one person was never actually reached. People sit beside each other for years without being known.

Nothing has caused more suffering over the millennia than the inability to do that one thing. Not the differences themselves — the failure to make contact across them.

That is what the group is for. Not agreement. Contact.
